A working beta with no design

The product already existed as a beta assembled straight from wireframes. The functionality worked, but there was no visual system and no patient-facing UX logic — screens spoke the language of the database rather than the language of someone waiting for a test result. Design started where the requirements ended.

The clinic's own staff work in the CRM: registration, doctor schedules, test referrals, treatment courses, diet and medication recommendations. Everything the patient sees in the app is a view of that same record.

Not another generic medical service — a continuation of the relationship between a specific clinic and its patient.

Four scenarios in the MVP

We kept the MVP to the needs that appear before, during and after a visit. The test for every candidate feature was blunt: can the patient handle this without calling reception or coming in for paperwork?

Self-registration

The patient creates an account with a phone number and an SMS code — no clinic staff involved. The lowest possible barrier to entry.

Visits, past and upcoming

Booking, date, doctor and status in one place: reminders for what is coming, an archive of everything already done.

Tests and treatment

Assigned and overdue tests, the treatment course with medications on a schedule, and results with trend charts over time.

Chat with the clinic

A direct line to the doctor or an operator — text, voice or a photo — with an assistant answering the routine questions from the patient's own record.

How the MVP was shaped

  • 01 User scenarios

    The moments a patient actually touches the clinic — before a visit, in the waiting room, after a referral, and in the weeks of a treatment course — written out as flows rather than screen lists.

  • 02 Navigation

    Five modules in the tab bar, and a home screen that answers one question first: what do I owe today. Everything else is reachable in one tap from there.

  • 03 Role model

    Who writes and who reads across the CRM and the app: clinic staff, doctor, operator and patient, with the chat as the only two-way surface in the MVP.

  • 04 Key system states

    Empty, assigned, overdue, completed, unpaid. Every list had to say what it wants from the patient next, in the same words the clinic uses on the phone.

  • 05 MVP scope

    Feature priorities agreed with the Product Owner: what ships in the first release, what waits for the second, and what the CRM keeps to itself.

A visual language for a clinic, not a hospital

The beta had no visual system at all. The one built for it is small on purpose, and two colours carry all the meaning — magenta is something you tap, acid is what is happening right now — while red is reserved strictly for money owed, never for a result. Two typefaces: a heavy geometric for everything a person reads, a mono for everything a machine wrote. Every component below is the live element lifted from the screens, not a redraw.
